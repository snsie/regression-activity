One Correction at a Time

How does each new tree improve the model, and when should it stop?

Your task
An interactive experience that lets a visitor follow successive corrections and choose a stopping round using validation performance.
Audience
An anonymous visitor who knows machine learning and regression, but not how gradient boosting builds a prediction.
Learning objective
Explain how boosting adds one small correction per round, and use validation error to choose when to stop.
Viewer takeaway
Boosting starts at the average. Each small tree fixes part of the remaining error, until more trees stop helping on new people.

Where your numbers come from

Everything is in outputs/<dataset>.json under boosting {{help model-outputs}}:

  • init: round 0, the training average.
  • people.<id>.staged[m]: the prediction after round m (501 values).
  • people.<id>.corrections[m-1]: what tree m added. This already includes the learning rate.
  • rmse.train, rmse.validation, best_validation_round: the error curves.

Common questions

Why start at the average?

Before any trees, the best single guess for everyone (with squared error) is the average outcome in the training data. Every tree after that corrects it.

Why are the corrections so small?

Only 10% of each tree's suggestion is used (learning rate 0.1). Small, careful steps usually generalize better than big jumps. {{help learning-rate}}

Why does training error keep falling when validation error doesn't?

Later trees start fitting noise: quirks of the training people that new people don't share. That's overfitting. {{help overfitting}}

Does every round improve every person?

No. A correction can move a person's prediction the wrong way, and a round doesn't have to improve validation error. Watch the overall error curves.

Why "validation", not "test"?

We look at the validation error to choose the stopping round, so it was used to make a decision. A true test result needs data that was never used for any choice. {{help test-result}}
