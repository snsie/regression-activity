Push & Pull

Which features push a prediction up or down, and by how much?

Your task
An interactive visualization that helps someone with no machine-learning background see how a linear regression model combines features {{help feature}} into a prediction {{help prediction}}.
Learning objective
Rebuild a prediction from a starting point and one signed push per feature, and tell a coefficient apart from one person's push.
Viewer takeaway
The model starts from an average prediction and adds one push per feature.

Where your numbers come from

Everything is in outputs/<dataset>.json under linear {{help model-outputs}}:

  • baseline: the starting point.
  • people.<id>.grouped_pushes and prediction: each person's pushes, with sex and ethnicity combined into one push each.
  • coefficients, reference (training averages) and typical_effect_per_sd.

For edited values, call m10.linearExplain(bundle, values) in the browser or m10.linear_explain(bundle, values) in Python. See using the model outputs.

Common questions

Why isn't the starting point the intercept?

The intercept is the prediction for someone whose every feature is 0 (age 0, BMI 0…), who doesn't exist. The starting point is the prediction for the training average, so each push shows how this person differs from average. {{help intercept}}

Why does a man, or a Non-Hispanic White person, still get a sex or ethnicity push?

Pushes are measured from the training average, which mixes men and women and every group. Being male moves you away from that mix. That's correct, so don't "fix" it. The reference group only describes how the 0/1 columns are coded.

The lung-function prediction for a child looks far too high. Is that a bug?

No. Lungs grow through childhood and shrink later in life, and one straight line can't follow both. Showing where the line fails is a strength of an explainer.

Can I say a higher BMI causes higher blood pressure?

No. The model learned patterns in survey data. Say "people with higher BMI tend to get higher predictions". {{help association}}
