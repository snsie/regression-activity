Submit & evaluation

At the end of the 105-minute build, submit your GitHub repository link on Canvas. Your README carries your app link. Then you'll spend 30 minutes trying classmates' apps and voting.

What to submit

On Canvas
The link to your GitHub repository, e.g. https://github.com/<username>/<repo>.
In README.md
At the top: your public app link, a direct link to each of the three sections, and one takeaway sentence per section. The starter's README has a place for each.

Make sure the repository is public, or that your instructor can open it. Your app is reviewed from the link in your README, so test that link in a private window.

After class, add the log of the prompts you gave your AI agent to your repository as prompts.md. That is the only change to make after you submit.

How voting works

  • You review 3 assigned apps, and each app gets 3 reviewers. Nobody reviews their own app.
  • After reviewing, vote for the app that best explained each model. Your three votes can go to different apps.
  • The 2 apps with the most votes per model go to the showcase, then the whole class votes. No self-votes.
  • Winners are decided by peer votes only.

Who can win

An app can be a finalist if:

  • its link opens without signing in;
  • all three sections work, each with a dataset menu;
  • it still shows the check.md numbers;
  • it makes no clearly false claim. For example: calling tree disagreement a confidence interval, using the intercept as the starting point, calling validation error a test result, or making causal claims.

This checklist only decides who can be a finalist; it adds no score. If a finalist has a problem, there may be a brief chance to fix it before the showcase.
