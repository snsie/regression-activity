Wisdom of the Forest

How do many different trees combine into one prediction?

Your task
An interactive experience that explains how individual decision trees {{help decision-tree}} contribute to a forest's combined prediction.
Audience
An anonymous visitor who knows machine learning and regression, but not how a random forest makes a prediction.
Learning objective
Explain how varied trees combine by averaging, and tell disagreement between trees apart from real uncertainty about a prediction.
Viewer takeaway
The forest asks 100 slightly different trees and averages their guesses.

How this section is judged

Where your numbers come from

Everything is in outputs/<dataset>.json under forest {{help model-outputs}}:

  • people.<id>.tree_predictions: all 100 guesses, in a fixed order.
  • people.<id>.prediction: their average, the forest's prediction.
  • bootstrap_unique_training_rows and features_tried_per_split: why the trees differ.

The helper m10.runningAverage(treePredictions, n) averages the first n trees. See using the model outputs.

Common questions

Why do the trees disagree?

Each tree learned from a different random sample of people and could only use a few features at each question, so each one learned slightly different rules.

Why is the average steadier than one tree?

One tree's quirks push its guess too high or too low. Across 100 trees, many of those mistakes point in different directions and partly cancel out. Steadier doesn't guarantee more accurate.

Isn't the spread of guesses a confidence interval?

No. The trees learned from overlapping data, so their spread isn't a calibrated measure of how far off the forest might be. Call it disagreement. {{help disagreement}}

Why can't the forest predict above the highest value it saw?

Each tree's guess is an average of training people's outcomes, so it always lies between the lowest and highest training outcome, and so does their average.
