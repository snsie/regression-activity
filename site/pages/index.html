Explain the Model

Build one app that explains three regression models {{help regression}} to an anonymous visitor: someone who knows about machine learning and regression, but doesn't understand how these algorithms work. Your classmates use your app as that visitor would, then vote for the section that best teaches each learning objective.

How your app is judged

Each section is judged on one question: how well does it teach its learning objective to the target visitor? Reviewers vote on that, not on how polished the app looks or how many features it has.

The visitor
Knows what machine learning and regression are. For example, a model learns from data to predict a number, and it should be checked on new data. They don't know how a linear regression, a random forest or gradient boosting turns a person's data into a prediction.
Anonymous
They open your link cold. They haven't seen this page, taken this class or met you, and you aren't there to explain. Everything they need has to be in the app.
Success
After a few minutes in a section, they can explain its learning objective in their own words.
SectionLearning objective: after using it, the visitor can…
Push & Pullrebuild a prediction from a starting point and one signed push per feature, and tell a coefficient apart from one person's push.
Wisdom of the Forestexplain how varied trees combine by averaging, and tell disagreement between trees apart from real uncertainty about a prediction.
One Correction at a Timeexplain how boosting adds one small correction per round, and use validation error to choose when to stop.
  • Teach the algorithm, not the basics. Don't spend your space on what machine learning is. Define a term briefly when you use it, then spend the space on how the model works.
  • Polish only counts if it helps. A plain section that teaches the objective beats a beautiful one that doesn't.
  • The required checklists are the minimum. Meeting them lets your app compete; they don't earn votes on their own.

Fair play

  • Use the supplied numbers. Don't retrain the models or invent predictions.
  • Work on your own. Your AI agent writes code; you make the design decisions and check the numbers.
  • Back up as you go. Commit and push to GitHub whenever a section works.
  • Freeze it after you submit. Don't change your app during evaluation unless your instructor approves a fix.
