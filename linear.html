Push & Pull

Which features push a prediction up or down, and by how much?

Your task
An interactive visualization that shows how a linear regression model combines features ? into a prediction ?.
Audience
An anonymous visitor who knows machine learning and regression, but not how linear regression builds a prediction.
Learning objective
Rebuild a prediction from a starting point and one signed push per feature, and tell a coefficient apart from one person's push.
Viewer takeaway
The model starts from an average prediction and adds one push per feature.

Common questions

Why isn't the starting point the intercept?

The intercept is the prediction for someone whose every feature is 0 (age 0, BMI 0…), who doesn't exist. The starting point is the prediction for the training average, so each push shows how this person differs from average. ?

Why does a man, or a Non-Hispanic White person, still get a sex or ethnicity push?

Pushes are measured from the training average, which mixes men and women and every group. Being male moves you away from that mix. That's correct, so don't "fix" it. The reference group only describes how the 0/1 columns are coded.

The lung-function prediction for a child looks far too high. Is that a bug?

No. Lungs grow through childhood and shrink later in life, and one straight line can't follow both. Showing where the line fails is a strength of an explainer.

Can I say a higher BMI causes higher blood pressure?

No. The model learned patterns in survey data. Say "people with higher BMI tend to get higher predictions". ?
